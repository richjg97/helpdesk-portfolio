Task 1 — Technical Issue Resolution

Incident Report — Ticket #54321

After resolving the GreenScape email issue through a series of diagnostic questions and guided troubleshooting steps, a formal incident report was completed documenting the full lifecycle of the ticket — from initial report through root cause identification, resolution, and closure.

Incident Details

Incident ID

#54321

Date and Time

May 19, 2026 — approximately 5:00 PM

Reported By

Jenna Marshall, Office Manager — GreenScape Landscaping

Incident Description

Brief Description

GreenScape Landscaping reported being unable to access their main business email account (support@greenscapelandscaping.com). Each login attempt caused the page to refresh without displaying any error message. The issue started late on May 19, 2026 with no prior changes to account settings or passwords.

Impact on Operations

With email being GreenScape's primary communication channel, the outage blocked all contact with clients and suppliers, disrupting scheduling, invoicing, and day-to-day operations.

Detailed Analysis

Steps Taken to Identify the Problem

  • 1.Reviewed the ticket and noted the key symptom: login loop with no error message.
  • 2.Contacted Jenna via email to gather further diagnostic details.
  • 3.Asked targeted questions to isolate the scope and root cause.
  • 4.Checked the email provider for known service outages — none found.
  • 5.Narrowed root cause to a browser-side issue based on client responses.

Key Findings

Issue was isolated to a single browser on one workstation. No server-side disruption. Conclusively identified as corrupted browser cache and cookies.

Resolution

Steps Taken

  • 1.Advised Jenna to clear Chrome's cache and cookies via Settings > Privacy and Security > Clear Browsing Data.
  • 2.Instructed her to close and reopen Chrome, then attempt login again.
  • 3.When the issue persisted, advised opening a Chrome incognito window and attempting login from there.
  • 4.Login via incognito was successful on the first attempt, confirming the root cause.

Resolution Timeframe

Approximately 45 minutes from initial contact, within Datacom's standard SLA for high priority tickets.

Outcome and Follow-Up

Client Satisfaction

Jenna replied via email confirming access had been restored, stating the steps were easy to follow. She thanked the team for the quick turnaround and expressed confidence in Datacom's support going forward.

Follow-Up Recommendations

  • Clear browser cache and cookies regularly — every two to four weeks.
  • Keep Google Chrome updated to the latest version.
  • Use incognito mode as a quick diagnostic step if a similar issue comes up before contacting support.

Ticket Closure

Ticket #54321 formally closed on May 20, 2026 following written confirmation of resolution from the client.

Task 2 — Customer Support and Escalation

Ticket Prioritisation Plan

A batch of five support tickets arrived simultaneously, including a critical network outage affecting two entire client organisations. The task required categorising each ticket, building a prioritisation plan using ITIL frameworks, and justifying every decision.

Ticket Categorisation

Ticket #001 — Network Outage — Global Design Inc.

Total loss of internet and cloud access affecting every user. Operations halted. Major incident.

Ticket #002 — Network Outage — Peak Solutions

Identical situation to #001. Full network failure across the entire office. Same root cause suspected. Treated as one major incident.

Ticket #003 — Service Request — Creative Solutions

Software installation request ahead of a project deadline. No work stopped. Planned task, not an incident.

Ticket #004 — Account Access — FinTech Advisors

Single user locked out of email with financial documents due today. Hard deadline makes this time sensitive.

Ticket #005 — Hardware Issue — BuildRight Construction

Laptop screen flickering affecting one user. Temporary workaround available via external monitor.

Prioritisation Plan

Critical

Tickets #001 and #002 — Network Outage

Logged as a single major incident. Two entire organisations losing connectivity simultaneously points to a shared infrastructure issue. Cannot be resolved at Tier 1. Immediate specialist escalation required following full ITIL incident management process.

High

Ticket #004 — Password Reset

Hard financial deadline today. Fix is fast and can be completed while the network escalation is already underway with the specialist team.

Medium

Ticket #005 — Hardware Issue

One user impacted with a workaround available. Same-day attention but not an emergency.

Low

Ticket #003 — Software Installation

No work stopped. Service request scheduled once urgent tickets are resolved.
